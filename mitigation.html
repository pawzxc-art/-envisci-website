Mitigation & solutions

We cannot refill the aquifer overnight, but we can slow the sinking.

Solutions work at three levels: what households and barangays do, what government does, and how we use water and land over the long term.

Three levels

Who can do what

Community actions

  • Use water wisely and fix leaks; less demand means less pumping.
  • Keep canals, drains and creeks free of garbage so tidal water can drain.
  • Plant and protect mangroves and trees along the shore.
  • Prepare a family emergency kit, evacuation route and a list of flood-safe contacts.
  • Report new cracks, tilting or unusual flooding to the barangay and on this site.
  • Join barangay flood watch and clean-up drives.

Government initiatives

  • Regulate groundwater. The National Water Resources Board (NWRB) has identified zones where groundwater use is restricted, a UP geologist reported.
  • Monitor the ground. Researchers recommend long-term satellite (InSAR) monitoring and real-time systems that feed early warnings into planning.
  • Plan land use with subsidence in mind and improve water-use data from utilities.
  • Build and maintain flood defences such as dikes, check gates and drainage, with open public reporting on progress.
  • Larger options like a coastal road or megadike have been proposed locally and need careful study.

Sustainable practices

  • Shift supply from wells to safe surface water where possible.
  • Harvest rainwater for washing, gardens and cleaning.
  • Reuse greywater where safe and efficient fixtures reduce use.
  • Use mangrove and nipa belts as natural wave and surge buffers.
  • Build and repair homes with raised floors and flood-tolerant materials.
  • Keep open space and soft ground that absorbs water instead of paving every plot.

These lists mix actions reported by officials with measures recommended by researchers. Where possible, each page cites its source; check the References page before treating any item as a policy that is already in force in Hagonoy.
