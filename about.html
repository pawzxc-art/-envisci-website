About land subsidence

The ground beneath us is not fixed.

Land subsidence is the gradual or sudden sinking of the Earth's surface. It can be natural, but in Bulacan human activity is making it faster.

Definition

What is land subsidence?

Land subsidence is the gradual or sudden sinking of the Earth's surface. Natural causes include tectonic activity and the slow compaction of sediments. Human activity, above all excessive groundwater extraction, can make it much worse (UP Resilience Institute).

It matters most near the sea. If the land goes down while the sea stays level or rises, the relative sea level goes up. That is what residents experience as "higher tides".

Key ideaSubsidence is not an earthquake or a sinkhole. In Hagonoy it is slow, widespread and measured in millimetres per year. That makes it easy to miss until floods keep returning.
Why here

Why river deltas are vulnerable

Hagonoy lies on the low delta where the Pampanga and Angat river systems meet northern Manila Bay.

Soft, young sediments

Deltas are built from layers of clay, silt and sand. Water-filled clay compacts easily when the water is removed.

Reliance on groundwater

Where piped surface water is limited, households, farms, fishponds and industry often pump from wells.

Almost no elevation

When land is only slightly above sea level, a few centimetres of sinking decide whether a street floods.
