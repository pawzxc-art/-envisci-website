FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is Hagonoy really sinking?
Yes. A satellite-radar study of Hagonoy and Calumpit measured sinking at several centimetres per year, with local peaks of about 124.5 mm/year between 2015 and early 2021. Rates vary from place to place.
Is it the same as a sinkhole?
No. A sinkhole is a sudden collapse of the ground. Subsidence in a delta is slow and covers a wide area, so you usually notice it through effects like flooding and cracks, not through a hole.
Why does pumping water make the land sink?
Water in the aquifer helps hold up the soil above it. When water is removed faster than it is replaced, the soft clay and silt compact. Most of that compaction is permanent.
Is sea-level rise the real cause of the flooding?
Both matter. The DENR says sea level in Bulacan is rising faster than the global average, and the land is also sinking, so the water reaches higher relative to the ground. Rain and river flow add to this.
Can the land be raised back up?
Generally not. Compacted sediments rarely rebound. That is why the goal is to slow or stop further sinking by reducing groundwater pumping and planning with subsidence in mind.
What can one household do?
Use water efficiently, fix leaks, keep drains clear, prepare for floods, join barangay activities and report observations. See the checklist on the Solutions page.
Is this website an official warning?
No. It is a student awareness project built from published studies and news reports. For emergency or official information, contact your barangay, the Municipal DRRMO, PAGASA or NDRRMC.
Where do the numbers come from?
Every figure is tied to a source on the References page. Rates come from different studies, periods and methods, so they should not be compared directly.
