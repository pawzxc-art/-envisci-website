What is happening

Flooding that no longer needs a storm

Hagonoy is a coastal municipality in the delta of Bulacan and Pampanga. It is flat, low, and crossed by rivers and fishponds. It has long flooded, but residents now describe water entering streets and homes at high tide, even on days without rain.

Researchers who measured the ground from space found that parts of Hagonoy and neighbouring Calumpit are sinking by several centimetres every year. The strongest link they found is groundwater pumping. When the land lowers, the same sea level reaches farther inland.

How it works

Four steps from a water pump to a flooded street

Water is pumped out

Wells draw groundwater faster than rain and rivers can refill it.

Sediments squeeze

As water pressure drops, the soft clay and silt under the delta compact.

The surface sinks

Compaction is mostly permanent. The ground does not bounce back.

The tide reaches farther

With lower land, ordinary high tides and river backwater flood homes and roads.
